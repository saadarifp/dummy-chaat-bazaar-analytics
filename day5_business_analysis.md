# Day 5 Business Analysis

CEO question: where should management focus attention?

## Three important findings
1. Revenue is concentrated. Karama alone produces 44.5 percent of company net sales, and Karama plus Muweilah produce 77.9 percent. Three products out of fifteen (Paneer Tikka, Pav Bhaji, Samosa Chaat) generate just over half of all revenue.
2. Talabat is the most expensive channel. It carries the highest order volume and charges the highest commission rate, taking AED 188.90 of the AED 322.85 total commission, 59 percent of all commission paid.
3. Barsha underperforms on value per order, not just volume. Its average order is AED 18.66 against Karama's AED 51.45. Its top seller is Sev Puri, a low ticket item, while high ticket Mains barely appear there.

## Two potential problems
1. Dependency risk. If Karama or Paneer Tikka weakens, roughly a third to a half of revenue is exposed. Nothing in the current mix cushions that.
2. Commission drag on growth. Delivery channels take 20 to 25 percent of gross. Growth driven mainly by Talabat grows costs nearly as fast as sales.

## Two recommendations
1. Push Direct ordering where delivery volume is highest. Every AED 100 moved from Talabat to Direct retains about AED 24 currently paid out.
2. Test the Karama playbook in Barsha. Barsha's problem is small baskets, so introduce combos pairing its popular chaat items with Mains and measure whether average order value moves toward the other branches.

## One thing I cannot conclude from this data
Profitability. The dataset contains no costs: no ingredients, rent, wages or delivery packaging. Karama has the highest net sales but I cannot say it is the most profitable branch, and I cannot say any product makes money. Net sales here means revenue after discounts and commission, not profit.

Also worth stating: with 35 dummy rows across one month, none of these findings are statistically reliable. The method is what matters; the conclusions must be re run on real data.
